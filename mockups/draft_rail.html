The Draft Rail

One real window from a real board, at the hardest moment in it. Every box below is an actual pick from Greatest Show on Paper 2 — not a sketch. The rail's job is to show the span the engine's waiting-cost numbers are already about.

The window is derived, not chosen

It runs my previous turn → my next turn, straight out of the pick order. Nobody picked “show 6 ahead.” Here that is 27 boxes; at this manager's next turn it is 2. A fixed count would be wrong at both.

The gap is the engine's own quantity

23 intervening picks. That is the exact span survival_probability, opportunity_cost, positional_forfeit and rival_premium integrate over. The rail is that number drawn.

Who is up ≠ whose slot it is

82 of 360 picks (22.8%) in this draft were made by someone other than the slot owner. Seven of them are in this window alone. A rail labelled by slot would be wrong about who is on the clock roughly a quarter of the time.

Two clocks, deliberately unalike

The league clock is Sleeper's truth. The debate lock is ours — the moment after which a call cannot come back in time to use. Same units, different authority, so they must not look alike.

It does not fit, and that is the point

27 boxes will not sit on one screen. So the rail pans, the edges dim under the side UI, and re-center snaps the anchor back. Scroll it and the button wakes up.

Nothing here round-trips

Panning, dimming and re-centering are the component's own business and never reach Python. Only making a pick and calling Debate do. That is what keeps this a game surface instead of a form.
